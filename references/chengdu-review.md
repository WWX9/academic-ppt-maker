# Chengdu 2025 Review Notes

Use this file when the user wants to learn from the Chengdu 2025 information geography conference corpus.

Focus on **report logic**, not decoration.

## Positive Examples

### `报告-杜思齐.pptx`

Why it is strong:

- Open with one explicit problem: spatiotemporal heterogeneity.
- Use `相关工作` only as a bridge into `思考 - 回到自然语言处理与生成式模型`.
- Keep the deck on one thesis from introduction through summary.
- Close with `假设 -> 方法设计 -> 验证`, which gives a clean academic ending.

What to learn:

- Use one core claim for the whole deck.
- Avoid repeating agenda or section reset slides.
- Let summary answer the same question raised near the opening.

### `面向色觉异常人群的无障碍地图研究进展.pptx`

Why it is strong:

- Use background and prior work to narrow into one explicit scientific problem.
- Move from `研究现状` to `科学问题` and then to `研究思路` without losing the audience.
- Use method results plus cognitive experiments to close the loop.

What to learn:

- Build a bridge from literature to one clear problem statement.
- Add behavioral or user evidence when the topic needs it.

### `信息地理学大会2025_蔡建南.pptx`

Why it is strong:

- Use a thesis-like repeated pattern: definition -> limitation -> new idea -> method -> case -> interpretation.
- Run the same clean logic twice for two related subproblems.
- Keep titles informative enough that the audience can follow the story from titles alone.

What to learn:

- Reuse the same logic skeleton for multiple sub-questions.
- Keep titles narrative, not generic.

### `信息地理学大会 刘宝举.pptx`

Why it is strong:

- Match each problem bottleneck to a visible method component.
- Separate two main difficulties instead of mixing them.
- Let simulation and real-case evidence appear in a controlled order.

What to learn:

- Map `problem A -> method A` and `problem B -> method B`.
- Use method slides to answer specific obstacles, not to enumerate techniques.

### `250820_谭惜文_基于不同驱动和参数化方案的地表能量通量模拟优化.pptx`

Why it is strong:

- Behave like a short conference talk template.
- State background, uncertainty, and research necessity very early.
- Keep method detail minimal and reserve space for result closure.

What to learn:

- For short talks, compress background and method.
- Make conclusion bullets point back to concrete earlier questions.

## Negative Examples

### `自然资源-王敬哲-第二届全国信息地理学大会.pptx`

Why it is risky:

- It works as a review lecture, but not as a tight research report.
- The deck branches into too many subtopics after identifying two key questions.
- The audience receives rich material but weak single-thread closure.

What to avoid:

- Turning a research talk into a topic encyclopedia.
- Expanding after the gap has already been defined.

### `吴森森-时空大数据智能分析方法及应用-v2.pptx`

Why it is risky:

- It is a成果总览 rather than one argument.
- The deck returns to the outline several times.
- It lists many methods and applications without one anchor question.

What to avoid:

- Treating a multi-project overview as a single-paper talk.
- Repeating agenda slides because the title chain is too weak to navigate by itself.

### `隧道场景灯具提取15：15.pptx`

Why it is risky:

- It over-expands method detail, especially around one algorithm block.
- The narrative drifts late in the deck by adding a new task line.

What to avoid:

- Letting implementation detail replace problem-method closure.
- Introducing a second story in the final third of the talk.

### `刘佳昕_EGLR.pptx`

Why it is risky:

- Background and contextual facts arrive before the true question is visible.
- Common-model explanation takes too much room.
- Results appear before the audience fully understands why this method is needed.

What to avoid:

- Teaching generic method knowledge inside the main talk.
- Delaying the research question too long.

### `cugb胡玉乾zs+.pptx`

Why it is risky:

- The title chain is weak and technical labels dominate.
- Several slide titles do not tell the audience why the slide exists.
- The deck tries to recover multiple lines of thought too late.

What to avoid:

- Using titles like `LOSS`, `静态特征`, or `27维属性` as stand-alone navigation.
- Leaving obvious residual or unrelated slides at the end.

## Distilled Rules

- Prefer titles that behave like claims or questions, not file names or variable names.
- Use agenda slides sparingly; strong title chains reduce the need for repeated navigation.
- End background with one explicit scientific question before expanding method details.
- Make each method module answer one previously defined difficulty.
- Order results to mirror questions or method modules.
- Keep the conclusion anchored to evidence and scope, not slogans.
- If the user explicitly names a presenter for visual taste, treat that deck as an appearance prior first, then separately choose a logic prior.
